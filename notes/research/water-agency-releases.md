# Research notes: reservoir releases

Working notes for understanding how the water agency manages releases on the Salinas River. Not published to the site — kept here for reference while we figure out what to surface to users (release schedules, expected flows, bypass requirements).

> During the period from around 2013 to 2023, there were more years with drier conditions and the reach of river from the reservoirs to Bradley generally showed some losses. ### Takeaways worth following up on

- **Release schedule is approximate.** It's a forecast of what's needed to meet downstream demand, not a fixed plan. Releases get adjusted in near-real-time based on actual diversions and channel losses.
- **Summer diversion target ---** up to 72 Acre-Feet/day at the Salinas River Diversion Facility (SRDF), when storage allows, plus required bypass to the lagoon.
- **Required bypass ---** ranges 2–45 cfs depending on water year type and other factors. Currently 15 cfs. 2026 is forecast wet-normal.
- **Releases respond to real conditions ---** driven by actual SRDF diversions and channel losses, plus weather (heat waves raise demand). Example: releases bumped +40 cfs one day and +60 cfs the next to reach 485 cfs total, prompted by Spreckels flow dropping and forecast heat.
- **Reservoir-to-Bradley reach ---** was generally a losing reach in the drier 2013–2023 period; since 2023/2024 it has sometimes been a gaining reach, consistent with historical wet-period observations. Relevant to how we model/forecast flow at downstream access points.
